Irrigation is the artificial application of water to crops to support growth when rainfall is insufficient.

Types of Irrigation:
Surface irrigation includes flood and furrow irrigation.
Drip irrigation delivers water directly to plant roots and reduces wastage.
Sprinkler irrigation simulates rainfall and is suitable for uneven land.
Subsurface irrigation applies water below soil surface.

Water Requirement Factors:
Crop type, growth stage, soil texture, temperature, and humidity influence irrigation needs.

Efficient Irrigation Practices:
Irrigate early morning or evening to reduce evaporation losses.
Use mulching to retain soil moisture.
Adopt micro-irrigation systems for water efficiency.
Avoid over-irrigation to prevent root diseases.

Signs of Over-Irrigation:
Yellowing leaves, root rot, waterlogging, nutrient leaching.

Signs of Under-Irrigation:
Wilting, leaf rolling, stunted growth, poor yield.

Modern Techniques:
Soil moisture sensors help optimize irrigation timing.
Automated irrigation systems reduce labor and water wastage.
