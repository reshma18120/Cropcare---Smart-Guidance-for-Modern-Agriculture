Vertical farming is a method of growing crops in vertically stacked layers using controlled environments.

Key Features of Vertical Farming:
Efficient use of space.
Controlled temperature, humidity, and lighting.
Minimal water usage through recirculation systems.
Suitable for urban and indoor farming.

Growing Systems:
Hydroponics uses nutrient-rich water.
Aeroponics sprays nutrients to roots.
Aquaponics combines fish farming with plant cultivation.

Crops Suitable for Vertical Farming:
Leafy greens such as lettuce, spinach, kale, and basil.
Herbs like mint, parsley, and coriander.
Microgreens and strawberries.

Lighting:
LED grow lights provide required light spectrum.
Light duration and intensity must be controlled.
Red and blue light wavelengths promote plant growth.

Climate Control:
Maintain optimal temperature and humidity.
Ensure proper ventilation and air circulation.
CO₂ enrichment improves growth rate.

Advantages:
Year-round production.
High yield per square meter.
Reduced pest and disease incidence.
Less dependency on climate conditions.

Challenges:
High initial setup cost.
Requires technical knowledge.
Dependence on electricity.

Best Practices:
Monitor nutrient solution regularly.
Maintain cleanliness to prevent diseases.
Use automation and sensors for efficiency.
