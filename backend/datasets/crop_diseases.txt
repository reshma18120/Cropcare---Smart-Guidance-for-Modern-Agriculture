Crop diseases are conditions caused by fungi, bacteria, viruses, nematodes, or environmental stress that reduce crop yield and quality.

Fungal Diseases:
Early blight in tomato causes brown concentric rings on older leaves. It spreads in warm and humid conditions. Control includes crop rotation, removal of infected leaves, and fungicide application.
Late blight affects potato and tomato, showing water-soaked lesions that turn brown. It spreads rapidly in cool, wet weather. Copper-based fungicides and resistant varieties are recommended.
Powdery mildew appears as white powdery growth on leaves of crops like cucumber, pumpkin, and grapes. It reduces photosynthesis. Control includes sulfur sprays and proper spacing.

Bacterial Diseases:
Bacterial leaf blight in rice causes yellowing and drying of leaf tips. It spreads through water and infected seeds. Use resistant varieties and balanced nitrogen.
Bacterial wilt in tomato and brinjal causes sudden wilting even with moist soil. Crop rotation and soil solarization help control.

Viral Diseases:
Tomato mosaic virus causes mottled, curled leaves and stunted growth. It spreads via tools and seeds. Infected plants must be removed and destroyed.
Leaf curl virus causes severe leaf curling and yield loss. Control whitefly population using yellow sticky traps and neem oil.

Nematode Diseases:
Root-knot nematodes cause galls on roots leading to poor nutrient uptake. Control methods include neem cake, marigold intercrop, and crop rotation.

General Prevention:
Use certified seeds, maintain field hygiene, avoid water stagnation, follow crop rotation, and monitor crops regularly for early symptoms.
