Hydroponics is a method of growing plants without soil using nutrient-rich water solutions.

Common Hydroponic Systems:
NFT (Nutrient Film Technique) uses a thin film of nutrients flowing over roots.
DWC (Deep Water Culture) suspends roots directly in oxygenated nutrient solution.
Drip systems deliver nutrients directly to plant roots.
Aeroponics sprays nutrients as mist to exposed roots.

Advantages of Hydroponics:
Hydroponics uses up to 90 percent less water than soil farming.
It allows year-round cultivation and faster plant growth.
It reduces pest and soil-borne disease problems.
Suitable for urban and indoor farming.

Crops Suitable for Hydroponics:
Leafy greens such as lettuce, spinach, basil, and coriander.
Fruit crops like tomato, cucumber, and strawberry with proper support.

Nutrient Management:
Hydroponic solutions must contain nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, calcium, magnesium, and micronutrients.
pH should be maintained between 5.5 and 6.5.
Electrical conductivity (EC) indicates nutrient concentration.

Common Problems:
Algae growth due to light exposure.
Root rot caused by low oxygen levels.
Nutrient imbalance due to incorrect solution mixing.

Preventive Measures:
Maintain proper aeration, regularly clean systems, monitor pH and EC daily, and use clean water.
