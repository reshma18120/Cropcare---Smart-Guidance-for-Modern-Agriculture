Organic farming is an agricultural system that relies on natural inputs and biological processes.

Principles of Organic Farming:
Avoidance of synthetic fertilizers and pesticides.
Use of organic manures, compost, and green manure.
Enhancement of soil fertility through natural means.
Promotion of biodiversity and ecological balance.

Organic Inputs:
Farmyard manure, vermicompost, green manure crops.
Biofertilizers like Rhizobium, Azotobacter, and PSB.
Biopesticides such as neem oil, neem cake, and Trichoderma.

Pest and Disease Management:
Use crop rotation to break pest cycles.
Encourage natural predators like ladybirds.
Use botanical extracts such as neem, garlic, and chili sprays.

Advantages:
Improves soil health and water retention.
Produces chemical-free and safe food.
Reduces environmental pollution.
Fetches premium market prices.

Challenges:
Lower initial yield compared to conventional farming.
Requires more labor and management.
Transition period before certification.

Certification:
Organic certification ensures compliance with organic standards and improves market value.
